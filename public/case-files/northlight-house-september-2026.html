Krepis · six-kernel back office · month-end case

Northlight House, September 2026

A twelve-person guesthouse in Reykjavík runs one full month through Krepis: hiring terms, the roster, clocked hours, payroll, housekeeping stock, supplier and wage payments, and the books. At the end, every figure matches an expected result that someone else worked out by hand beforehand.

12 staff 180 eight-hour shifts 14 payments settled 6 kernels, one database Atlantic/Reykjavik, UTC+0

Northlight House, its staff and its supplier are fictional. The pay rules are invented test policies, not Icelandic labour or tax law. Amounts are whole krónur, written the Icelandic way: 4.125.040 kr.

The house and its rules

One role, house duty, covers reception, guest help and housekeeping. Every day has three windows: day 06:00–14:00, evening 14:00–22:00 and night 22:00–06:00, with two people on each. Four staff are leads at 3.200 kr. an hour and eight are attendants at 2.400 kr.

Night minutes earn a 25% supplement. Staff pay 4% of gross into the staff pension, and the house adds 11,5%. Nobody may work twice in one civil day, and there must be at least eleven hours between shifts. The books open on 1 September with 8.000.000 kr. in the bank against the owner's capital.

How the month moves through the system

Each kernel owns one part of the business. They hand work to each other directly; nobody re-types anything between them.

  1. Chreos · agreements

    Employment terms

    Twelve employment agreements set each person's start date, grade and pay group. Staffing and payroll both read them from here.

  2. Taxis · rostering and time

    Roster, then clocks

    180 shifts are proposed and confirmed against the rest rules. Clock-ins and clock-outs then become worked time, including a mid-month swap and a late correction.

  3. Misthos · payroll

    Payroll for September

    Worked minutes are priced against the wage table, night band, pension deduction and employer contribution. The run is previewed, checked against the expected figures, then committed.

  4. Apotheke · stock

    Housekeeping supplies

    Two deliveries of five items from Hekla Hygiene ehf., daily consumption, a stock count, and a closing valuation.

  5. Obolos · payments

    Paying everyone

    Twelve wage payments, one combined pension remittance and one supplier payment go out on 3 October through a sandbox payment rail. A payment only counts once the rail confirms it.

  6. Zygos · the books

    The general ledger

    Payroll, stock and settlements post themselves into one operating book. Running a posting step again adds nothing.

What changed mid-month

Real months are not tidy. Each of these was recorded as a correction on top of the original, which stays in the history.

16 September

Shift swap

Birkir's evening shift went to Katla. The roster amendment was confirmed against the rest rules, and payroll followed it without anyone touching pay.

10 September

Late clock-out

Aron's day shift actually ended at 14:12. The correction replaced the old time record, and he is paid for the extra 12 minutes.

Deliveries

Wrong product codes

Ten deliveries were booked against supplier SKUs instead of the stock system's own item IDs. They were re-pointed at the right items in place, without receiving the goods twice.

Stock count

Two rolls short

The count found two fewer rolls of guest toilet paper than the records showed. A 370 kr. shortage was booked separately from ordinary use.

How we know it is right

One team wrote the business facts and worked out the expected results before any software ran. A second team operated the system without seeing those answers. A third compared the two.

  • Payroll, the ledger, stock quantities and stock value all match the hand-calculated expectation. The independent checker reported 4.913 comparisons with no differences.
  • A double-booked shift was refused by the roster's overlap rule, and a journal off by 1 kr. was refused by the ledger's balance rule. Neither left a trace in the records.
  • Sending a payment to the rail does not post anything. Only the rail's confirmation does, so 14 confirmations produced exactly 14 postings.
  • Re-running every handoff and resending the payroll and receiving requests changed nothing.
  • The whole system was restarted. All six kernels' records, including every event log, balance, stock level and payment record, were identical before and after.

The first attempt failed, usefully

The first run on 2 October came out 42.000 kr. too high on gross pay. Payroll had counted all of Elín's and Sigrún's 30 September night shift in September, including the six hours each that fell in October. The expected figures were not adjusted to fit. Instead the run exposed five gaps, each fixed in the product:

  • Splitting a shift that crosses midnight at the payroll period boundary.
  • Reading pay terms straight from the employment agreements instead of re-entering them.
  • Correcting a delivery that cited the wrong item, without receiving it twice.
  • Posting a supplier payment to the right book automatically.
  • Clearing a combined pension payment through one account, with no manual reversal.

The rerun on 3 October used the same source facts, unchanged, and every figure matched.

Not covered yet

This month is the hourly, single pay group case. Four limits found during review are known and still open:

  • Two separately configured rule sets can reuse the same rule ID, and one may be silently dropped.
  • A hand-written placement that cites the wrong agreement can still be reported as clean.
  • After manual rebinding, the pay-terms import can check a different rule version from the one payroll uses.
  • For salaried staff, a later termination date can shorten pay without a warning. Hourly pay, as used here, is not affected.
